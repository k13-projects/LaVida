Overall Verdict

Compliance Level: Good — One Required Fix

The La Vida website is in strong shape on accessibility and is unusually privacy-friendly — it runs no analytics, sets no tracking cookies, and stores no customer data on its own servers. There is one required fix: the catering form collects personal information (name, phone, email), which under California law means the site must publish a Privacy Policy. It does not have one yet.

What Actually Applies

The Rules Governing This Site

A single-location restaurant marketing site with a catering enquiry form. The real framework is the ADA (via the website "nexus" to the physical restaurant), the WCAG accessibility guidelines courts rely on, and California's privacy and civil-rights statutes.

RequirementApplies?Where We Stand
Section 508 (Rehabilitation Act)Not ApplicableBinds federal agencies & fund recipients — not a private restaurant.
ADA Title III — physical venueMandatedRestaurants are "public accommodations." Premises accessibility is assessed separately from the website.
ADA Title III — websiteReachableVia the 9th-Circuit "nexus" rule (Robles v. Domino's). WCAG is the de-facto benchmark.
WCAG 2.1 Level AALargely ConformsSkip link, visible focus, AAA-level olive contrast, reduced-motion support. A few minor items remain (below).
Unruh Civil Rights Act (CA)MitigatedTurns any ADA gap into $4,000+/violation. Risk lowered by the strong accessibility baseline.
CalOPPA privacy policyRequired — MissingThe catering form collects name, phone & email. CalOPPA requires a published privacy policy whenever a site collects personal info from California residents.
CCPA / CPRALikely Below ThresholdA single-location restaurant is unlikely to meet CCPA's revenue / data-volume thresholds, but the privacy-notice duty still applies.
Cookie consent bannerNot RequiredNo analytics and no tracking cookies were found; no GDPR-style banner needed in the US.
CAN-SPAMNot TriggeredNo newsletter or promotional email program on the site.
Already Solid

What's Working In Your Favor

  • ✓
    Strong accessibility baseline — skip-to-content link, visible focus rings, AAA-level contrast on the olive brand color, semantic landmarks, labelled form fields, and full prefers-reduced-motion support.
  • ✓
    Privacy-friendly by default — no Google Analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising cookies. There is very little personal data to govern in the first place.
  • ✓
    Data minimization — the catering form opens the visitor's own email app (mailto) — nothing is stored on a La Vida server, and there is no database to breach.
  • ✓
    Payments offloaded — ordering and card payment are handled entirely by Toast (pickup) and Grubhub (delivery); the site never touches payment data, which keeps PCI scope at zero.
  • ✓
    An Accessibility Statement page already exists — linked in the footer — documented good-faith effort, which helps against Unruh/ADA demand letters.
Open Items & Watch-List

What To Address

  • ⚠
    Privacy Policy — required, currently missing  ·  The catering form collects name, phone & email. Publish a privacy policy that discloses what's collected, the mailto handling, and the third parties the site touches (Behold Instagram widget, Google Fonts, Google Maps links, Toast, Grubhub). Link it in the footer.
  • ⚠
    Terms of Service — recommended  ·  A short ToS with ordering terms and a liability disclaimer is good practice for a restaurant site.
  • ⚠
    Security headers are thin  ·  Only a basic upgrade-insecure-requests CSP is set. Add HSTS, X-Frame-Options (or frame-ancestors), and a real Content-Security-Policy via a deploy headers file. See the companion security-audit.md.
  • ⓘ
    Minor accessibility polish  ·  add lang="en" to the <html> tag, an aria-live="polite" region for the auto-advancing reviews carousel, and alt text on the order button image.
  • ⓘ
    Attorney sign-off not obtained  ·  This review is thorough but is not a legal opinion. A short California-attorney review of the new privacy policy closes the loop.
